EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
In T20 cricket, standard metrics like raw strike rate or economy hide critical context. An average of 140 SR in relaxed middle overs does not reveal who can chase 36 off 12 balls or defend 10 in the final over. This project builds a mathematically rigorous, domain-grounded Pressure Reliability Framework. Every delivery across 883 historical IPL and T20I matches (2022–2026) is assigned a continuous Pressure Score (0–1) based on required run rates, wickets in hand, death-phase leverage, and tournament stakes. Using an 18-cell context-expected matrix, player performances under high pressure are compared against their personal baselines and match-to-match consistency. The system delivers two definitive, unrepeated Top-10 lists ready for Unstop dropdown submission.

2 How Pressure is Defined and Quantified

Pressure is not a binary switch; it is a continuous dynamic function of match resources, phase criticality, and consequence. We model delivery pressure separately for the chase and the setting innings:

2.1 Innings 2: The Chase Pressure Dynamics

In a run chase, target information is deterministic. Pressure is dictated by three compounding forces: Required Run Rate urgency, endgame proximity, and wicket scarcity:

P_chase = 0.45 · RRR_factor + 0.30 · (RRR_factor · Endgame_factor) + 0.25 · Wicket_factor

Where: RRR_factor = min(1.0, max(0.0, (RRR - 6.0) / 10.0)) captures required boundary frequency; Endgame_factor = (120 - Balls_Remaining) / 120 reflects dwindling recovery time; Wicket_factor = (10 - Wickets_in_Hand) / 9 penalizes tail exposure.

2.2 Innings 1: Target-Setting Pressure Dynamics

Batting first presents two acute pressure environments: Early Collapse Crisis (where losing 3+ wickets in Powerplay or 4+ wickets before over 10 threatens being bowled out) and Death Overs Execution (overs 16–20 where boundary maximization is mandatory):

  • Collapse State: If wickets fallen ≥ 3 in overs 1–6 or ≥ 4 before over 10: \(P = 0.65 + 0.05 \cdot \min(5, ext{Wickets} - 2)\).
  • Death Phase: Overs 16–20: \(P = 0.60 + 0.10 \cdot ( ext{Over} - 16) / 4.0\).
  • Super Overs (Innings 3 & 4): Instantaneous elimination: \(P = 1.0\).
  • Knockout Match Multiplier: In Finals, Qualifiers, Eliminators, and Semi Finals (`knock_out == 1`), every ball carries heightened tournament leverage: \(P \leftarrow \min(1.0, P imes 1.15)\).
Validation of High Pressure Threshold (P ≥ 0.60)
Filtering the 200,999 deliveries at \(P \ge 0.60\) isolates 51,211 deliveries (25.5%). Empirical evidence confirms this cleanly separates ordinary play from crisis: league batting average crashes from 27.90 to 18.91 (−32.2%), bowling strike rate accelerates from 21.04 to 14.19 balls/wicket (+48% faster dismissals), and economy rises from 8.48 to 9.17 rpo.

3.1 Value Added Formulas

For every ball, player performance is evaluated against the exact expectation of that context:

  • Batting Total Value Added (TVA): \( ext{TVA} = \sum ( ext{Runs} - E[ ext{Runs}]) + 12 imes \sum (E[ ext{Dismissal}] - ext{Dismissal})\). Each avoided dismissal is credited at 12 runs, representing the marginal value of wicket conservation in T20.
  • Bowling Total Value Saved (TVS): \( ext{TVS} = \sum (E[ ext{Bowler Runs}] - ext{Bowler Runs}) + 12 imes \sum ( ext{Bowler Wickets} - E[ ext{Bowler Wickets}])\).

6 Match Consistency Checks & Model Reliability

To verify that ratings were not skewed by one-off freak performances, we conducted rigorous consistency testing across individual matches:

  • Match-by-Match Net Impact: Every player's performance in each match's high-pressure deliveries was quantified as \(NMI = ext{Value Added} - ext{Dismissal Penalty}\).
  • Win Contribution Rate (WCR): The percentage of high-pressure matches where the player produced a positive Net Match Impact (\(NMI > 0\)). Top-10 batters average a 62.4% WCR, while league average is 46.1%.
  • Empirical Bayes Regularization: By shrinking small samples towards the league prior (\( ext{Cred} = N / (N + 50)\)), we eliminate flash-in-the-pan batters who struck at 220 across 20 balls.
Model Limitations and Nuances
1. The Baseline Ceiling Effect (e.g. Jasprit Bumrah): Bumrah's baseline economy is 6.73 rpo (best in cricket history). When bowling high-pressure death overs, his economy is 7.08 rpo. In raw delta terms (\(6.73 - 7.08 = -0.35\)), this looks like a dip, but against the IPL death expectation (9.45 rpo), he saves a staggering 187.1 runs. Our Total Value Saved (TVS) metric properly captures his +188 value.
2. Batting Order Bias: Top-order anchors (e.g. Kohli, Gill) face only 15–20% of their balls in high pressure, whereas dedicated finishers (Rinku, David, Stubbs) face 50–75%. Our volume credibility factor prevents low-exposure openers from dominating finishing lists.
